# Writer's desk

**Idea.** The project is a desk seen from above: the manuscript is a sheet of paper, scenes are index cards in a recessed well, scene details are a card clipped to the page, and navigation is a library card catalog. Every group is a separate physical object with its own soft shadow, so "what belongs together" is answered by "what is the same object" and no hairlines are needed.

**Grouping.** Elevation and material carry all the grouping. The cabinet (wood, brass label holders) is navigation; the sunken well holds the scene cards, with manila divider cards for chapters and "Unfiled"; the paper is the one big object; the clipped card holds scene metadata; references are a separate little stack of tagged cards below it. In settings, each group is its own folder with a labeled tab (Project info, Custom theme, Tags, Spell checking), spaced apart on the desk. Inside an object, grouping is only whitespace (plus one red index-card rule under the scene ID).

**Emphasis.** Four levels, each a different face as well as a different weight: user data in Literata serif at full ink (prose, scene and chapter titles, field values, outline, notes); labels in Atkinson Hyperlegible, smaller and brown-grey; real metadata in IBM Plex Mono (`SCN-007`, `1,240 W`, `Edited 12 min ago`, `Chapter 1`); empty things are dashed outlines with faint text and no fill or shadow ("Add a word", "Add a tag", "New scene", "Add a reference"). Location is stated once: the pulled-out drawer (Story / Project settings) says where you are, the open scene card is pulled out of the well toward the page, and the scene title is a heading only on the paper (the clipped card carries the ID, not the name again).

**Distinctive.** The pulled-out drawer as the "you are here" signal, the open card sliding toward the sheet, the binder-clipped metadata card, and toggles and keys with real travel. Dark theme is the same desk at night: a banker's lamp at the top right throws a warm pool across the desk, and all shadows fall down and to the left, away from it (by day they fall straight down). No textures anywhere: wood, brass and paper are flat colors and gentle gradients, and nothing behind text is patterned.

**Biggest risk.** Kitsch and cost. The metaphor is one step from 2010 skeuomorphism, and it spends a lot of pixels on furniture (cabinet, well, clip) on a 1280 px screen; on a phone the cabinet and well must collapse into something else, and the metaphor may not survive that. Shadow-based grouping also depends on a display and eyesight that can see soft shadows; in dark mode in particular, the separation between objects is subtler than it looks on a good monitor.

## Phone (`?device=phone`, 412 x 915)

Same desk, but the furniture changes shape. The card catalog lies along the bottom as five drawer fronts; the current drawer is pulled up toward the page, with the dark cavity showing beneath it. Project settings is a small drawer knob beside the nameplate. The card tray turns into a row of index cards tucked behind the top edge of the sheet: the open card stands taller, and its neighbors and chapter dividers peek out on either side. The card-box button opens the whole tray as a wooden box lifted onto the desk (`state=scenes`). The clipped card shrinks to a stub clipped to the sheet's corner (`SCN-007`, `1,240 W`); tapping it lifts the full card, with references, over the page (`state=meta`). The tool strip keeps eight 44 px keys (the list key holds both list types, and Focus and Reader are the two manila keys). Settings is a vertical stack of the same folders; `state=spell` scrolls to Spell checking.

Phone risk: the strip of tucked cards scrolls sideways and shows only about three cards, so "where am I in the book" comes from the box rather than at a glance.
